Research

Research agenda

My work asks how artificial intelligence reorganizes persuasive communication, information environments, and the relationship between human judgment and computational systems.

01 · AI & Persuasion

I examine when generative AI can persuade, how personalization changes persuasive outcomes, and whether AI systems exhibit systematic differences in the persuasive strength of content generated for different positions or audiences.

Key questions
What makes AI-generated persuasion effective? When does personalization add value beyond generic messages? How do model behavior and audience characteristics interact?

02 · Algorithms & Information Environments

I use algorithm audits, social bot field experiments, and cross-platform comparisons to study recommendation systems and conversational AI. A recurring concern is to distinguish genuine algorithmic amplification from bounded personalization, platform constraints, and persistent algorithmic memory.

Key questions
Do algorithms amplify user preferences, merely reflect them, or sometimes suppress them? How should causal claims be made when platforms are dynamic and partially observable?

03 · AI-Mediated Communication

I study how people understand, trust, and respond to AI-generated information, especially where uncertainty and social consequences matter. Current domains include health information, fact-checking, counter-speech, morality, and human–AI interaction.

Key questions
When does AI replace human interaction, and when does it facilitate it? How does answer-based communication alter uncertainty, confidence, and subsequent information-seeking?

Methods

My research uses online and field experiments, surveys, computational content analysis, qualitative interviews, mixed methods, social network analysis, and algorithm auditing. I am particularly interested in designs that connect controlled causal identification with ecologically valid platform behavior.
